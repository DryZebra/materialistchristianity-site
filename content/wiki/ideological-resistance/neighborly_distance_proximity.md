# Neighborly Distance: The Strength of Being Close

**Neighborly Distance** is the technical rule that says your neighborhood is only as strong as the people you can actually reach out and touch. In a world obsessed with the "Digital Ghost" (the internet and social media), Materialist Christianity focuses on the **Physical Reality** of the street you live on.

## 1. Physical Trust vs. Digital Lies

You can’t synchronize with someone you only see on a screen. 

*   **The Range of the Hand**: Real work—the kind that keeps you fed and warm—happens within **Walking Distance**. If the grid goes down, the guy in the next state can’t help you fix your generator. Your neighbor can.
*   **The Hollowing of Social Life**: Globalist companies want you to "Connect" with people thousands of miles away because it’s easier to sell you stuff that way. They want you to ignore the family next door. That’s how they hollow out a town’s strength.

## 2. Local Synchronization

Trust isn’t built through a "Message." It’s built over the fence, in the breakroom, and on the shop floor. 

*   **The Structural Bond**: When you see your neighbor every day, you know if they're reliable. You know if they do what they say. That physical closeness is the "Glue" (the structural bond) that makes a neighborhood strong enough to survive a collapse.
*   **The Kingdom is Local**: Christ didn't build a digital platform; he walked from village to village. He worked with the people who were **Right There**. 

## 3. Summary

To be a Materialist Christian is to reclaim your neighbor's name and pick up the tools in your own street. The Kingdom of God isn't in the cloud; it's in the physical proximity of people who have decided to survive together.
